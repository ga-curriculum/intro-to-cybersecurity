# Common Threats in the Wild

There are multiple types of attacks within cybersecurity and the list is always evolving. Bad Actors are creating new ways to break in just as quickly as we are finding ways to stop them. Here are a few of the typical security issues that can be seen in the wild. 

1. Malware

Malware includes many different varieties of digital disasters. Here is a list of different types and a quick summary of what they do:

- Ransomware (locks your stuff)
- Spyware (spies on you)
- Viruses (spreads through files)
- Trojans (pretends to be good)
- Worms (spreads by itself)

2. Social Engineering

Social engineering is the most popular threat around! It's very common and has a very high success rate. This is generally bad actors trying to use people to get the information they want, but shouldn't ave. Here is a list of tactics you should be aware of:

- Phishing (fake emails)
- Vishing (voice phishing)
- Smishing (SMS phishing)
- Pretexting (creating fake scenarios)
- Baiting (leaving infected USB drives)

3. Network Attacks

While there are a number of tech focused attacks that happen on the daily, the most common attacks are listed below with a brief description of what occurs.

- Man-in-the-Middle (eavesdropping)
- DDoS (overwhelming services)
- Password attacks (guessing passwords)
- Evil twin (fake WiFi networks)

## Real-world Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The Coffee Shop Trap

Maria is working at a coffee shop and needs to check her bank account. She connects to "Free_Coffee_WiFi" without checking if it's legitimate. A hacker created this network to steal information!

**What went wrong?**

- No verification of network
- Using banking on public WiFi

When using technology in public, be extra careful! These might not be things that everyone thinks about, but they're definitely things a bad actor looks for! Be cautious to avoid any malware reaching your system. 

### Scenario 2: The Email Oops

John gets an urgent email from his "boss" asking him to buy gift cards for a client. He rushes to help... but it wasn't really his boss!

**Red flags:**

- Urgent request
- Unusual ask
- Pressure tactics

This is the perfect example of a social engineering attack. Be careful when you're taking requests at work and make sure they're actually coming from the parties you think they are! It's okay to ask questions, it's better to be safe than sorry. 

**Remember:** Security is a journey, not a destination. Keep learning and stay safe!
